Annisa’s Indonesia Blog

Paragraph 1August 17 is a public holiday here in Indonesia because this is when we celebrate Hari Merdeka, which is Indonesian Independence Day. Indonesia declared its independence from the Netherlands on August 17, 1945 and then fought for four years until the Netherlands accepted the declaration. We celebrate this historic event in lots of different ways in every town and village.

Paragraph 2Before the holiday, people prepare by decorating the streets and houses in red and white, our national colors, and preparing kerupuk, which are traditional snacks. Students also practice marching in school. On the actual day, there are formal flag ceremonies and marching processions to remember our national heroes. There are also less formal events, like games, races, and competitions.

Paragraph 3Panjat pinang, which is the hardest competition, involves climbing up a very slippery pole to get prizes at the top. It’s great that we have fun on this day, but I also think it’s good to remember what happened in the past. The snacks, for example, remind us that the country was poor, and panjat pinang reminds us of the struggle for independence. All in all it’s a happy day and an exciting time to visit the country if you’re a tourist!

05 · Explain who and what

Essential or extra?

Join two sentences

I respect people.

They defend our country.

I respect people who defend our country.

The relative clause comes after the word it describes. Here, who replaces they. Do not use both: say who defend, not who they defend.

Defining

Which people or things?

The students who joined the race were excited.

The clause tells us which students: the students in the race.

No commas. Without the clause, we do not know which group of students the writer means.

Non-defining

What extra information?

Annisa, who writes this blog, explains the holiday.

We already know who Annisa is. The clause gives us extra information about her.

Use commas. Use two commas if the clause is in the middle of the sentence. Use one comma before a clause at the end.

Choose the relative word

People or things?DefiningNon-defining
Peoplewho / thatwho
Things and ideaswhich / thatwhich
CommasNoYes
Remember: English punctuation depends on the type of relative clause. A defining clause has no commas: I respect people who help others.
More relative words

Whose shows possession: a student whose father is a teacher.
Where describes a place: the town where I live.
When describes a time: the day when we met.

In a defining clause, you can leave out the relative word when it is the object: the blog (which) I read. Keep it when it is the subject: the blog which explains the tradition. In a non-defining clause, keep the relative word.

Which and that

Which can describe things in both types of clause.

We read a blog which explains the tradition.
Kerupuk, which are traditional snacks, are popular.

That can replace who or which in a defining clause. It cannot be used in a non-defining clause.

We read a blog that explains the tradition.
Not: Kerupuk, that are traditional snacks, …

A · Fill the gaps

Use who, which or that. Sometimes more than one answer is possible. Choose D (defining) or N (non-defining).

1. who / that — D. 2. which — N. 3. which / that / no relative word — D. 4. who — N.

In sentence 3, the relative word is the object, so it can be left out. Do not use that in sentences 2 and 4.
